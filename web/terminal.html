How the score is built

Conviction = 100 × (Q · C · R)1/3, where each pillar is built from cross-sectional percentile ranks rather than absolute thresholds.

Q — Quality (ranked within GICS sector)

  • ROIC — NOPAT ÷ (debt + equity − cash), NOPAT = operating income × (1 − 21%)
  • FCF yield — (operating cash flow − capex) ÷ market cap
  • Gross margin — gross profit ÷ revenue, reconstructed from cost of revenue where a filer omits the tag
  • Leverage — net debt ÷ EBITDA, ranked inverted
  • Earnings stability — 1 − coefficient of variation of annual net income

Ranking within sector is deliberate: a grocer's 25% gross margin is excellent for a grocer, and ranking it against software would turn the quality score into an unintended sector bet.

C — Confirmation

Excess return versus SPY at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months (weighted 0.15/0.25/0.30/0.30), divided by realised volatility, plus a trend term for price relative to its 50- and 200-day averages. Dividing by volatility is what makes a utility's +6% comparable to a semiconductor's +18%; without it a momentum ranking is a high-beta screen in disguise.

A name whose quality percentile clears 0.55 and which trades more than 15% below its 52-week high receives a mean-reversion uplift of up to 30%. The quality gate is the point — an identical uplift applied to a low-quality name rewards a falling knife.

R — Risk

Dollar liquidity (average daily traded value), valuation (earnings yield and EBITDA/EV, ranked within sector), and realised volatility ranked inverted. Yields rather than multiples, so the measure stays finite and correctly ordered through zero and negative earnings.

Why the geometric mean

A product of three sub-unit factors puts a thoroughly median company at 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 ≈ 12/100, which places every name below a BUY threshold of 70 — the tiers describe a structure the arithmetic cannot produce. The cube root restores the scale while keeping the property that matters: a pillar near zero still vetoes the score, because you cannot recover from a zero factor.

Data, claims and limits

Missing data

An input the filings do not report is imputed at the sector median percentile and lowers the name's data confidence, which is reported alongside the score. Absent evidence is not negative evidence: scoring an unmeasured company as 0 emits a confident sell recommendation on the basis of nothing.

What the Monitor tab does and does not claim

The Monitor tab grades the pipeline, not the predictions. Freshness, dispersion, coverage, ranking stability and specification continuity all measure whether the machinery is working. A board can pass every one of them and still forecast nothing: stability says the ordering is not being reshuffled by noise, it says nothing about whether the ordering is correct.

The question the Monitor tab cannot answer — do high-conviction names outperform low-conviction names — is an Information Coefficient measurement. It needs months of accumulated nightly snapshots, and it has to be computed within a single specification hash, because a scoring change mid-series makes the two halves different datasets. That is why every snapshot records the hash of the frozen specification that produced it, and why the specification history is displayed rather than buried.

Sources

  • Fundamentals — SEC EDGAR XBRL company facts. Every figure traces to an accession number, linked from each name's detail panel.
  • Prices — Nasdaq daily OHLCV, with Yahoo and a committed cache behind it. All price features are derived locally from one series.
  • Macro — FRED (10-year Treasury, 2s10s, VIX, high-yield OAS).

Limitations

  • Fundamentals are as-filed and lag the market by up to a quarter. Trailing twelve-month figures roll the last fiscal year forward using the most recent reported quarter where both legs share an XBRL tag.
  • Dual-class companies (e.g. BRK.B) report share counts as dimensional facts that the bulk frames API omits, so market cap can be missing. It is shown as missing rather than guessed.
  • Net debt / EBITDA is not meaningful for banks and insurers; those names take the sector median and a lower confidence score.
  • Percentiles are relative to this universe. A conviction of 80 means top of this list, not an absolute valuation claim.
  • This is a research tool, not investment advice.
