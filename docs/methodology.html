Methodology

Equity Conviction Monitor · model v3 · US large caps and factor ETFs

Conviction = 100 × (Q · C · R)1/3

Three pillars, each built from cross-sectional percentile ranks rather than absolute thresholds. A percentile asks "where does this company sit against its peers?", which adapts to margin and rate regimes, guarantees the score discriminates, and degrades gracefully when an input is missing. An absolute threshold asks "is ROIC above 25%?", which makes the score partly a statement about the number 25.

1 · Q — Structural quality

Ranked within GICS sector.

Ranking within sector is deliberate. A grocer's 25% gross margin is excellent for a grocer; ranking it against software would convert the quality score into an unintended sector bet, and the terminal would simply recommend software every day.

Q = 0.02 + 0.98 × q — the floor is non-zero because a company in the bottom percentile of its sector is a poor holding, not a nonexistent one, and mapping it to exactly zero discards all information below the floor.

2 · C — Market confirmation

Excess return versus SPY at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months, weighted 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.30 / 0.30, then divided by realised volatility. Longer horizons dominate because 12-month momentum is the durable effect while 1-month is largely reversal.

Dividing by volatility is what makes a utility's +6% comparable to a semiconductor's +18%: it asks how many units of risk the outperformance took. Without that step a momentum ranking is a high-beta screen in disguise.

A trend term (price relative to its 50- and 200-day averages, weighted 0.4 / 0.6) contributes 30% of the pillar and separates "cheap and recovering" from "cheap and still falling".

The quality mean-reversion uplift

A name whose quality percentile clears 0.55 and which trades more than 15% below its 52-week high receives a confirmation uplift, scaling to +30% at a 50% drawdown.

The quality gate is the entire point. An uplift applied to every name in a drawdown rewards a falling knife exactly as much as a quality business on sale — which inverts the strategy this terminal is named after.

3 · R — Risk and price

InputDefinitionWeight
Dollar liquidity63-day average of close × volume0.40
Valuationmean of earnings yield and EBITDA/EV, ranked within sector0.35
Low volatilityannualised realised volatility, ranked inverted0.25

Yields rather than multiples: a yield stays finite and correctly ordered through zero and negative earnings, where P/E flips sign and becomes actively misleading in a ranking. The measure is two-sided, so cheapness is rewarded rather than merely un-penalised.

4 · Why the geometric mean

Multiplying three sub-unit factors puts a thoroughly median company at 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 ≈ 0.125, i.e. 12 out of 100 — below a WATCH threshold of 40, let alone a BUY threshold of 70. Published tiers then describe a structure the arithmetic cannot produce.

The cube root restores the scale (a median name lands near 58) while keeping the property that made the model multiplicative in the first place: a pillar near zero still vetoes the score, because there is no recovering from a zero factor.

5 · Missing data

An input the filings do not report is imputed at the sector median percentile and lowers the name's data confidence, which is reported beside the score and drives the Data quality tab.

Absent evidence is not negative evidence. Scoring an unmeasured company as zero emits a confident sell recommendation on the basis of nothing — which is itself a fabricated signal, and precisely how the previous version of this model rated all 48 of its names AVOID.

6 · Portfolio construction

Only names scoring 55 or better are held. Weights are proportional to conviction above 40, capped at 5% with the overflow redistributed. Weighting the entire universe — including names the model rates AVOID — produces a market-cap tracker with extra steps.

Trailing twelve months

TTM = FY + Q_latest − Q_same_quarter_prior_year, computed only when all three legs resolve to the same XBRL tag. Filers tag the same concept differently across periods, and subtracting one tag from another yields a plausible number that is quietly nonsense.

8 · Verification

  • Ledger validator — fails the build on a degenerate result: conviction dispersion below 5, fewer than three populated tiers, a constant pillar, implausible market caps, or per-field coverage below its floor.
  • Parity gate — the terminal recomputes conviction in the browser, so the scoring function exists twice. The test extracts the JavaScript port out of the terminal, runs it in node, and asserts agreement with Python across 400 randomised inputs plus edge cases.
  • Regression tests — each documented v2 failure has a named test asserting it cannot return.

9 · Limitations

  • Fundamentals are as-filed and lag the market by up to a quarter.
  • Dual-class companies report share counts as dimensional facts the bulk API omits, so market cap can be missing. It is shown missing rather than guessed.
  • Net debt/EBITDA and FCF yield are not meaningful for banks and insurers; those names take the sector median and a lower confidence score.
  • ROIC is suppressed where invested capital is negative or near zero, since the ratio there carries a confident sign and no meaning.
  • Percentiles are relative to this universe. Conviction 80 means top of this list, not an absolute valuation claim.
  • Backward-looking returns shown in the Portfolio tab are not a track record.
